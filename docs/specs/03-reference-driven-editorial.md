# GENERALIST DAILY — REFERENCE-DRIVEN EDITORIAL INTELLIGENCE

## Objective

Replace generic topic selection and elementary educational explainers with a reference-informed editorial discovery system.

Generalist Daily is a current, intellectually stimulating publication for curious adults, primarily in their 20s and 30s.

Its central purpose is to deliver meaningful recent developments across the world, technology, business, science, society, and culture, complemented by carefully selected historical, philosophical, scientific, and other educational discoveries.

Freshness and editorial relevance should be central to the publication.

Evergreen knowledge should enrich the edition, not dominate or dilute its news value.

## Reference Research

Build a reference registry covering at least 30 diverse editorial publications, newsletters, and educational video channels.

Start with:

1440, The Knowledge, The Browser, Morning Brew, Axios, Semafor, Vox, The Economist, The Atlantic, The Pudding, Quanta Magazine, Aeon, NEWNEEK, UPPITY, LongBlack, Miracle Letter, TLDR News, Johnny Harris, Cleo Abram, ColdFusion, Asianometry, Veritasium, Kurzgesagt, TED-Ed, Nerdwriter1, Wendover Productions, and PolyMatter.

Expand the registry based on actual editorial relevance and source availability.

Do not assume every candidate is accessible.

Collect and analyze a representative sample of recent publicly accessible content, initially targeting 300–500 metadata records where feasible.

Do not copy or reproduce copyrighted articles, newsletters, or video transcripts without appropriate rights.

Store source URLs, metadata, short permitted excerpts, and original editorial analysis.

## Reference Analysis

For each suitable example, analyze:

- Subject and subtopic
- Publication date
- News freshness
- Editorial angle
- Why the topic is interesting
- Why it matters now
- Assumed reader knowledge
- Explanation depth
- Narrative structure
- Evidence quality
- What makes the content worth reading

Derive reusable editorial principles from multiple sources.

Do not imitate a single publication's distinctive voice or reproduce its wording.

## Daily Editorial Selection

The default edition should emphasize meaningful recent developments.

Begin with approximately:

- 3–4 timely stories covering important news, emerging technology, business, science, culture, or society
- 1–2 worthwhile educational or discovery-oriented stories
- 0–1 reflective, historical, or philosophical story when editorially justified

This is a flexible starting point, not a rigid quota.

Evergreen subjects should be chosen for exceptional interest, relevance, educational value, or a compelling connection to contemporary developments.

Do not force artificial connections between stories.

Do not select elementary definitions as standalone stories unless a significant current development makes them particularly relevant.

## Editorial Standard

Write substantive, accessible Korean journalism for adults.

The reader should learn something new, understand the relevant context, and leave with meaningful knowledge.

Avoid:
- Generic introductory textbook topics
- Headline-only summaries
- Random trivia
- Artificially simplified explanations
- Excessive technical depth
- Sensationalism
- Formulaic writing

Preserve the distinction between factual reporting, analysis, and interpretation.

## Implementation

1. Audit existing topic-selection logic.
2. Build a structured reference registry.
3. Collect permitted reference metadata.
4. Analyze editorial patterns.
5. Create a reusable editorial playbook.
6. Integrate the playbook into daily candidate evaluation.
7. Track subject diversity and freshness.
8. Generate a sample edition.
9. Compare its topics and writing quality against the current implementation.
10. Document what improved and what remains weak.

Reference material is for editorial guidance, not a substitute for independent reporting or factual verification.

The final system must produce original, source-grounded content rather than paraphrased copies of reference publications.
